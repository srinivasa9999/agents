Look at the old-habit trade again: it finished at basically breakeven, which makes it look harmless. It wasn't. At the 11:00 high, that averaged short was carrying an unrealized loss of roughly ₹7,700 — 1.5× the entire 1% risk budget the plan allows for one trade — with no stop and no plan to cut it. It only "worked" because the stock happened to pull back into the close. That's the mechanism behind "sometimes it works fine, then huge loss": the near-miss and the blowup are the same trade, decided by whether the pullback arrives in time. You cannot know that in advance, which is the entire argument for a stop.

3 · Why >50% win rate is still the wrong target

Forty simulated trades per style. Averaging into every extended move wins 70% of the time (+0.4R average) but its losers average −1.8R, and twice per cycle a "this has to turn around" position runs to −5R or worse — exactly the shape of the ₹7,700 drawdown above, just without a pullback to bail it out. The plan wins only 45% of the time and still finishes ahead, because every loss is capped at −1R and every win is +2R.
