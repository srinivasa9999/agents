Fade it, or follow it? HYUNDAI, 31 July 2026

Last Thursday, Hyundai Motor India opened well above its previous close and finished +7.2% — despite weak Q1 results. It is exactly the day the “it can’t go beyond ±4%” instinct gets punished. Below: what fading + averaging costs on this session, what the plan’s Setup A does instead, and why the win-rate you asked about is the wrong lever.

1 · The session, with both playbooks marked on it

Red markers: the old habit — short at +4% because “it won’t go further”, then average the loser twice as it keeps climbing, forced out at the close. Green markers: the plan’s Setup A — no trade in the first 15 minutes, join the move on the first pullback that holds above VWAP, stop below the pullback low, done at +2R by midday.

Price (1-min, illustrative path) VWAP Fade-and-average shorts Setup A long

The stopped-clock problem in one picture: the shorts were “right” that the stock was extended — it still never came back. Price held above ₹2,090 all day, so every averaged short only deepened the hole. Setup B (disciplined fade) correctly produced no trade here: the move never reached a pre-marked pivot/OI level and never printed a reversal trigger — standing aside was the right output, not a missed trade.

The point is not that the plan trade won — some days it stops out for −₹4,940 and that is a good trade. The point is the asymmetry: the fade’s loss had no ceiling, the plan’s loss is fixed before entry. That asymmetry, repeated over 40 trades, is the next chart.

3 · Why “>50% win rate” is the wrong target

Forty simulated trades per style. The fade-and-average style wins 70% of the time (+0.4R average win) but its losers average −1.8R and twice per cycle a “it must come back” trade runs to −5R or worse. The plan wins only 45% of the time — and finishes ahead, because every loss is exactly −1R and every win +2R. Expectancy, not win rate, decides.

Plan: 45% wins · +2R / −1R · expectancy +0.35R Fade & average: 70% wins · +0.4R / −1.8R avg, fat left tail · expectancy −0.47R
