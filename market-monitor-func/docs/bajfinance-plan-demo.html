Rule 7 costs you the fastest leg of this move on purpose: in the first 3 minutes alone the stock ran from ₹1,095 down to ₹1,083.60 and back — an 11-rupee round trip before 09:18. By 09:30, when trading is allowed again, ₹1,095→1,114 of the move (roughly two-thirds of the eventual gain) had already happened. That's the trade-off: you miss the cheapest entry, but you also miss the coin-flip whipsaw nobody can read in real time.

2 · Setup B check: was there a fade trade here at all?

Setup B requires a move running into a pre-identified level — a pivot, prior-day high/low, or top-OI strike. Bajaj Finance's previous session (30 Jul) had a high of ₹1,068.00. This stock opened at ₹1,095 — already ₹27 above that level, before the session even started. There was no level left to fade into.

Correctly applied, Setup B produces no trade here — not a missed opportunity, the right output. A move with no overhead level to reject from is not a fade candidate; it's the definition of "the middle of nowhere" the plan warns about in rule 4B.

The short's final loss (−₹4,671) understates what it felt like to hold. At the 14:27 high, that averaged short was carrying an unrealized loss of roughly ₹7,820 — over 1.5× the plan's entire per-trade risk budget — for most of the afternoon. Unlike the Hyundai session, this stock never pulled back far enough to bail it out. Same mechanism, different ending: this is the "huge loss" day, not the "got lucky" day.

The plan trade also didn't need the day's exact high to work: price kept running to ₹1,150 by 14:24 before the pullback, so a trader trailing stops below higher lows past 1.5R (rule 4A) could reasonably have banked closer to +2.4R. +2.00R is what the stated minimum rule guarantees, not the ceiling.
